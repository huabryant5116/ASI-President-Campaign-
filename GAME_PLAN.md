# ASI President Campaign: Game Plan

> **Assumptions:** "ASI" is Associated Students, Inc. at a CSU-style campus. Elections are in spring (filing around February, voting in March or April). The 3-month goal is to be in a strong, declared position by late February 2027. Update this plan with school-specific details: election timeline, current role, likely opponents, and top issues.

## Phase 0: Late November, get the rules
1. **Get the ASI Elections Code and bylaws** and read every page. Note:
   - The eligibility rules: minimum GPA, units enrolled, standing, and any required prior ASI service.
   - The filing window, the petition signature count, and any mandatory candidate orientation.
   - The campaign start date. **Campaigning before the official start is often a violation.** Before that date, build relationships but don't ask for votes.
   - Spending caps, receipt and disclosure rules, and rules on materials (chalking, posting, digital ads, use of club email lists).
   - Whether candidates run solo or on a slate with a VP.
2. **Meet the Elections Commissioner or ASI advisor** and ask clarifying questions. It shows you're serious and protects you from disqualification.
3. **Check your own eligibility**: GPA, units for spring, any conduct holds.

## Phase 1: December, listen and position
4. **Listening tour.** Hold 30–50 one-on-one meetings with:
   - Leaders of the large orgs: Greek councils, cultural orgs, athletics, academic clubs, residence hall associations, transfer and commuter groups.
   - Current ASI board members, including the outgoing president.
   - Key staff: the ASI advisor, student life staff, the basic needs coordinator.

   Ask what's broken, what ASI has failed to deliver, and who they trust. These notes become your platform.
5. **Map the electorate.** Turnout is usually low (often 5–15%), so you win by mobilizing organized blocs. Identify the 8–12 groups that make up most of the likely voters.
6. **Draft a 3-point platform.** Make it concrete, fundable, and inside ASI's actual authority: fees, programming, advocacy to administration, and student services. Avoid promises ASI can't keep.
7. **Recruit your core team of 5–8 people:**
   - a campaign manager
   - a social and design lead
   - a volunteer and outreach lead
   - a compliance and treasurer person
   - people with ties to different communities

   If there's a slate, choose a running mate with complementary networks.

## Phase 2: January, build the machine
8. **Collect endorsement commitments privately.** Get verbal yeses from org leaders now, timed to go public at launch. Check whether the code restricts org endorsements.
9. **Build your assets:** brand, logo, slogan, headshots, a 60-second video, a one-page platform, and a website or Linktree. Hold them until the official start if required.
10. **Build a volunteer list and a schedule.** Aim for 30–60 volunteers, each committed to a few hours a week during the campaign.
11. **Budget.** Know the cap, keep every receipt, and fundraise only in the ways the code allows.
12. **Prepare for the debate.** Learn the ASI budget, the student fee structure, current campus controversies, and the outgoing administration's record. Run mock Q&As with tough questions.

## Phase 3: February, file and launch
13. **File early and correctly.** Turn in signatures with a 20–30% buffer, and attend orientation.
14. **Launch strong** on day one with endorsements, the video, and a visible presence. Momentum in the first 48 hours shapes how people see the race.
15. **Run the ground game.** Talk to students in person, visit class presentations if allowed, and speak at org meetings.
16. **Get out the vote.** During voting week, focus on getting committed supporters to actually vote. Track who has committed.

## Things that decide close races
- **Compliance can sink you.** Disqualification and sanctions decide many student elections. Document everything and ask before any gray-area tactic.
- **Your record counts for more than promises.** Deliver one visible win before February to prove what you can do.
- **Relationships with administration matter.** Show you can get things from the university.
- **Don't burn bridges.** Run positive, but contrast clearly on records and specifics.
- **Commuters, transfers, and grad students** are often ignored. Groups like these can make up your margin.
- **Be careful with digital spending and messages.** Many codes regulate paid ads, mass emails, and group-chat blasts.
- **Protect your grades.** Your eligibility and your stamina depend on them.

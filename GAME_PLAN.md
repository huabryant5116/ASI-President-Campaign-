# ASI President Campaign: Week-by-Week Checklist

> **Assumptions:** "ASI" is Associated Students, Inc. at a CSU-style campus. Elections are in spring (filing around February, voting in March or April). The 3-month goal is to be filed, endorsed, and ready to launch by late February 2027.
>
> **Important:** Once you have the official election calendar, shift these weeks to match it. **Don't ask for votes before the official campaign start date.** Until then, build relationships and prepare.

---

## December: Rules and listening

### Week 1 (Nov 30 – Dec 6): Get the rules
- [ ] Download the ASI Elections Code, bylaws, and the official election calendar
- [ ] Read the whole code and write down:
  - [ ] Eligibility rules: GPA, units, standing, and required prior service
  - [ ] Filing window and petition signature count
  - [ ] Mandatory orientation dates
  - [ ] Official campaign start date and what counts as "campaigning"
  - [ ] Spending cap and receipt and disclosure rules
  - [ ] Rules on posters, chalking, digital ads, mass email, and group chats
  - [ ] Solo vs. slate (running mate) rules
- [ ] Email the Elections Commissioner or ASI advisor to request a meeting
- [ ] Confirm your own eligibility: GPA, spring units, no holds
- [ ] Start a contacts spreadsheet with name, org, role, notes, and support level

### Week 2 (Dec 7 – 13): Start the listening tour
- [ ] Meet the Elections Commissioner or advisor and ask your gray-area questions
- [ ] Map the electorate: list the 8–12 orgs or blocs most likely to vote
- [ ] Schedule 10+ one-on-ones before finals (org presidents, ASI board members)
- [ ] Complete your first 5 one-on-ones. At each, ask what's broken, what ASI hasn't delivered, and who they trust
- [ ] Identify 2–3 people who could be your campaign manager

### Week 3 (Dec 14 – 20): Finals (keep it light)
- [ ] **Prioritize grades.** Your eligibility depends on them.
- [ ] Complete 3–5 more one-on-ones if time allows
- [ ] Send thank-you messages to everyone you've met
- [ ] Ask your top campaign manager pick to join

### Week 4 (Dec 21 – 27): Break, reflect
- [ ] Review your listening notes and group them into recurring themes
- [ ] Research the ASI budget and student fee breakdown
- [ ] Review the outgoing administration's promises and results

### Week 5 (Dec 28 – Jan 3): Draft the platform
- [ ] Draft 3 platform pillars that are concrete, fundable, and within ASI's authority
- [ ] For each pillar, write down the problem, your fix, how you'd pay for it, and who you'd work with
- [ ] Sketch a slogan and brand direction
- [ ] List the core team roles you still need to fill

---

## January: Build the machine

### Week 6 (Jan 4 – 10): Recruit the core team
- [ ] Lock in your core team of 5–8 people:
  - [ ] Campaign manager
  - [ ] Social and design lead
  - [ ] Volunteer and outreach lead
  - [ ] Compliance and treasurer (owns the elections code)
  - [ ] 2–4 people connected to communities you aren't
- [ ] Choose a running mate if it's a slate, ideally someone with networks that complement yours
- [ ] Hold the first team meeting: roles, goals, and a weekly meeting time
- [ ] Set up a shared drive, a team group chat, and a budget tracker

### Week 7 (Jan 11 – 17): Test the platform and build relationships with administration
- [ ] Test the platform draft with 5–10 people from your listening tour
- [ ] Meet 1–2 administrators (dean of students, VP student affairs) to learn what's feasible
- [ ] Revise the platform based on the feedback
- [ ] Draft your one-page platform

### Week 8 (Jan 18 – 24): Spring semester starts, endorsements
- [ ] Re-confirm spring enrollment and units
- [ ] Ask org leaders privately for endorsement commitments (target 5+), checking the code's endorsement rules first
- [ ] Complete 10 more one-on-ones with groups you haven't reached (commuters, transfers, grad students)
- [ ] Pick one visible quick win you can deliver before February, and start it

### Week 9 (Jan 25 – 31): Build assets
- [ ] Finalize the logo, colors, and slogan
- [ ] Take headshots and team photos
- [ ] Script and film a 60-second campaign video
- [ ] Build the website or Linktree, kept unpublished until the official start if required
- [ ] Create a volunteer sign-up form and recruit your first 15 volunteers
- [ ] Have your compliance lead review all materials against the code

---

## February: File and get launch-ready

### Week 10 (Feb 1 – 7): Prepare to file
- [ ] Get the candidate packet and petition forms
- [ ] Plan the signature drive: who collects, where, and when, aiming for a 20–30% buffer
- [ ] Draft the campaign budget within the cap
- [ ] Deliver or announce your quick win
- [ ] Reach 30 volunteers

### Week 11 (Feb 8 – 14): Signatures and debate prep
- [ ] Collect petition signatures with the buffer
- [ ] Double-check that the signatures are valid (enrolled students, legible, correct form)
- [ ] Hold mock debate #1 with tough questions ("What have you actually done?", "How will you fund this?")
- [ ] Lock in endorsements, targeting 8–12 orgs or leaders

### Week 12 (Feb 15 – 21): File
- [ ] **File early.** Don't wait for the deadline.
- [ ] Attend mandatory candidate orientation
- [ ] Get written confirmation that your filing is complete and valid
- [ ] Hold mock debate #2
- [ ] Build the launch-day plan (hour by hour, first 48 hours)

### Week 13 (Feb 22 – 28): Ready to launch
- [ ] Finalize the launch materials: video, endorsement announcements, website, posts
- [ ] Get your compliance lead's final sign-off on all materials
- [ ] Assign volunteer shifts for launch week and the first two weeks
- [ ] Build the get-out-the-vote list of everyone who has said they support you
- [ ] Prepare a voting-week plan: daily text or ask reminders and tabling locations
- [ ] **Goal reached: filed, endorsed, staffed, and ready to launch on day one**

---

## After the 3 months: Campaign and voting weeks
- [ ] Launch on the first allowed day with endorsements, the video, and a visible presence
- [ ] Do daily in-person outreach, class presentations if allowed, and visits to org meetings
- [ ] Attend every debate and forum
- [ ] Voting week: focus entirely on getting committed supporters to vote, and track who has voted
- [ ] Keep every receipt and file all required disclosures on time

---

## Things that decide close races
- **Compliance can sink you.** Document everything and ask before any gray-area tactic.
- **Your record counts for more than promises.** Deliver one visible win before you file.
- **Relationships with administration matter.** Show you can get things from the university.
- **Don't burn bridges.** Run positive, but contrast clearly on records and specifics.
- **Commuters, transfers, and grad students** are often ignored. Groups like these can make up your margin.
- **Be careful with digital spending and messages.** Know the rules on ads, mass emails, and group chats.
- **Protect your grades.** Your eligibility and your stamina depend on them.
